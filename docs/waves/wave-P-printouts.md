# Wave P: printouts (PDF reforming)

The owner's decision (19/11/2026): printouts are **HTML templates turned into PDFs by Chromium**, as in the owner's
NAVY-PAYROLL application. CIRERMS's own PDF conventions stay (the crest, the header, the footer with "RESTRICTED"
and "Page X of Y", the colours); NAVY-PAYROLL's PDFs are the standard for improving on them. In the app a PDF shows as real
pages in our own preview (PDF.js draws the pages; never the browser's PDF viewer and its toolbar).

Two packages, one after the other: D4 designs every printout as an HTML template in that design, and the owner
reviews them as real PDFs; after the owner approves (by merging), WP13 makes the app render its PDFs from those
templates with Chromium and rebuilds every printout the app makes today. Every later package then adds its
printouts as templates the same way.

Read `docs/waves/README.md` first: its rules and checks are binding for both packages.

**The reference.** NAVY-PAYROLL is public: `git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/hicadsystems/navy-payroll`
(read only). Study:
- `templates/*.html`: one Handlebars template per printout (for example `personnel-report.html`,
  `payslip-template.html`, `payroll-register.html`, `salary-summary.html`, `nhf-report.html`): how the page is set
  up (`@page`), laid out and spaced, the type sizes, the tables, the totals and the footers. Learn the quality from
  them; keep CIRERMS's own branding (below).
- `lib/chromiumPdfGenerator.js`: how the server turns HTML into PDF with Chromium (one shared browser kept open
  while busy and closed when idle, a small pool of tabs, waiting for fonts, A4, margins, timeouts, a size limit on
  the HTML), and `lib/pdfGate.js`.
- `controllers/Reports/payrollRegisterController.js` and `reportsFallbackController.js`: how a template is filled
  with Handlebars and handed to the generator.
- `package.json`: `puppeteer`, `puppeteer-core`, `@sparticuz/chromium`, `handlebars`.

### Design
1. **The house style: CIRERMS's own PDF conventions kept, and improved using NAVY-PAYROLL as the standard**
   (the owner, 19/11/2026: keep the logo, the page numbering and the other conventions as they are now; only
   improve on them). **Keep exactly**, as `src/shared/pdf.js` draws them today:

   - the footer on every page: "RESTRICTED — Nigerian Navy CIRERMS | Generated by: <the person's name, as the app

   - the **watermark**: the crest, faint, behind every page's content (`body::before`, fixed, full page height,
     centred, no repeat, `opacity: 0.02`, behind the text), using CIRERMS's crest (`public/assets/navy-logo.png`);
   - the **faint blue page background**: `@page { background-color: #f8fbff; }`;
   - the **fonts**: Inter for the text, Libre Baskerville for the headings and titles (Georgia as the fallback),

     the SIL Open Font License: keep their font files in the folder (for example from the `@fontsource` packages or
     Google Fonts), loaded locally, not fetched from the internet when a PDF is made (NAVY-PAYROLL fetches them;
     CIRERMS must not).
   **Improve**, taking NAVY-PAYROLL's templates as the standard of quality: spacing and alignment, the type sizes
   and hierarchy, A4 portrait or landscape chosen per printout, facts as label and value rows, tables (the header
   band repeated on every page, rows never split across pages, numbers right-aligned, totals rows), signature
   blocks where the old forms had them, density and legibility (also when printed in black and white). Any other
   change to a convention kept above is proposed in your report, not made.
   Put it in one shared stylesheet, `docs/design/printouts/printout.css`, and shared partials (`header.hbs`,
   `footer.hbs`, `signatures.hbs`), with the crest and any font as files in the folder (nothing fetched from the
   internet when a PDF is made). In Chromium the footer and "Page X of Y" come from the PDF footer template, so they
   appear on every page.
2. **Every printout as a Handlebars template**, `docs/design/printouts/<name>.hbs`, with a sample data file
   `<name>.sample.json` (believable data, at least one that runs past one page): the app's seven above; D1's

   Report (All Personnel), Disposition Report); D3's (Special Draft Form, Special Draft Letter, Report on
   Appraisal). The three verification forms keep D2's content until the owner supplies the real forms.
3. **A build script**, `docs/design/printouts/render.js` (`npm run printouts:render`), that fills each template
   with its sample data and renders it to `docs/design/printouts/pdf/<name>.pdf` with Chromium (Playwright's
   Chromium is enough for this design step; the app's renderer is WP13's). Commit the PDFs: they are what the owner
   reviews.
4. **README**: a "Printouts" section with the house style and a table of every printout: its template, the page and
   button that print it, orientation, sections, fields, and the package that wires it into the app.
